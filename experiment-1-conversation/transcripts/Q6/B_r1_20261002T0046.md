## Final answer

S-104's next ship date is **7 Oct 2026**, on PO005791 for 4,200 units of RM-221 (Polysorbate 80 NF, low-peroxide). If that PO slips, the exposure is the **cyclosporine dry-eye franchise**: three commercial packs with **13,986 TRx in the last 13 weeks** (3 Jul to 25 Sep 2026). Eight health systems account for most of that volume, and five of them have supply contracts.

**What makes this a real risk**
- **S-104** is Delmar Specialty Chemicals, a tier-2 supplier in India. It shipped 32.3% of its 96 closed POs late (ratified `late_ship_rate`).
- **No backup source:** S-104 is the only qualified source for RM-221. The alternate supplier, S-131, isn't qualified. The next step in the chain, the emulsifier premix CMP-310, also has a single source (S-201).
- **Slow recovery:** from when RM-221 ships, it takes about **50 days** for finished Lacrivia 5 mL to reach the DC. That includes a 14-day sterility test (BR-LEAD-01). So an on-time 7 Oct shipment reaches the DC around 26 Nov at the earliest, and every day of delay pushes that out a day. I only pulled lead times for Lacrivia 5 mL; the other packs share the same upstream steps.
- **No substitute:** the approved substitute for Lacrivia is Cyclosporine OS, but it depends on the same RM-221 supply (BR-SUB-01).
