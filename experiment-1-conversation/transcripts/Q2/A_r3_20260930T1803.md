   - PA expired and can't be renewed: 500 (21%), the largest single reason. It is rising, from 3.5 to 3.9 per 100 claims compared with the prior 13 weeks. This points to problems at renewal, not just first approval.
   - PA required: 439 (18.5%)
   - PA denied: 433 (18.3%)
2. **Not covered or not on formulary: 22% of rejections (513 claims)**, also rising, from 3.5 to 4.0 per 100 claims. These are concentrated in a few payers (see the next section).
3. **Everything else: about 20%**, spread thinly. It includes refill-too-soon and drug-review rejections (7%), days-supply and plan limits (6%), other administrative reasons (5%) and pharmacy-network restrictions (2%).

**Where the rejections come from**
- **By channel:** Health Exchange 27.6%, Managed Medicaid 23.6%, Medicare Part D 19.9%, Commercial 17.7%. Commercial still has the most rejections by volume (1,216).
- **By PBM:** Vantage PBM has the highest rate (25.1%) and 272 of the 513 coverage rejections. OptiScript has the most PA rejections (496).
- **Payers to prioritize:**
  - **Coverage gaps:** Evergreen Health Plans Part D (59.8% rejected, 100 coverage rejections); Harborview Health exchange (50.5%) and commercial (23.2%, 94 coverage rejections); Granite Plains Health Managed Medicaid (54.4%).
  - **PA burden:** Northstar Health commercial (25.9%, 230 PA rejections); Bluecrest Mutual commercial (133); Redwood Benefit Trust (119).

**What this means for market access:** LACRIVIA has two separate problems that need different fixes.
- **PA:** easier renewals or longer approval periods at OptiScript and Northstar, plus hub and field support for resubmitting expired PAs.
- **Coverage:** formulary negotiations with Vantage (Harborview, Redwood), Evergreen and Granite Plains.

**Sources and definitions**
- **Data:** the claims file, the product list (our products are the three brands flagged as company products), the rejection-code reference table and the plan-to-payer/PBM mapping (all Snowflake). I didn't need the supply-chain data.
- **Rejection rate:** rejected claims divided by paid plus rejected claims. Reversed claims are left out of the denominator; including them changes rates by less than 0.4 points and doesn't change the ranking.
- **Time window:** weeks run Saturday to Friday, matching the weekly prescription data, so the last 13 weeks are 27 Jun – 25 Sep 2026. I left out 26–30 Sep because only paid claims had arrived for those days, which would make rejection rates look too low.
- **Other assumptions:** results are at brand level with NDC packs rolled up. Cash/discount claims have no payer, so they have no rejections. The data is aggregated to product and payer level, with no patient- or prescriber-level detail, which fits market-access use.
