## Final answer

**Answer:** Lacrivia (cyclosporine 0.1%) had the highest claim rejection rate of any company product in the last 13 weeks: **18.4%** (2,147 rejected out of 11,664 paid plus rejected claims). The 10 mL pack was the worst single pack at **20.8%**; the 5 mL pack was 17.2%. Prior authorization rejections are the main driver, followed by formulary exclusions at a few specific plans. The rate has stayed between 17% and 20% every week since January, so this is a long-running access problem, not a recent spike.

| Product | Rejection rate |
|---|---|

| Lacrivia 5 mL | 17.2% (1,330 / 7,735) |

For context, Lacrivia's rate is about the same as its direct competitor Tearsol (18.1%), and much higher than Cyclomist (9.6%) and generic cyclosporine (4.8%).

**What's driving Lacrivia's rejections**
1. **Prior authorization: 58% of rejections** (1,241 claims, 10.6 of the 18.4 points).
   - The largest single reason is "PA exhausted / not renewable" (454). That points to PA renewals for continuing patients, not just first-time approvals. "PA denied" (394) and "PA required" (393) are close behind.
   - These rejections are concentrated in a few plans:
     - **Northstar Health Commercial (OptiScript):** the biggest single source, with 250 rejections (25.5% rate), 210 of them PA.
     - Bluecrest Mutual Commercial: 123 PA rejections.
     - Redwood Benefit Trust Commercial: 104.
     - Keystone Medical Plan Part D: 74.
     - Lakeshore Health Managed Medicaid and Part D: 72 and 60.
2. **Not covered / off formulary: 22% of rejections** (466 claims, 4.0 points). About 78% of these come from three payers:
   - **Harborview Health (Vantage PBM):** 202 across its plans. Its Health Exchange plan rejects 50% of Lacrivia claims.
   - **Evergreen Health Plans Part D:** 85, with a 58% rejection rate.
   - **Granite Plains Health:** 76, including a 54% rejection rate in its Managed Medicaid plan.
3. **Smaller groups:** refill-too-soon and drug review edits (6.6%), days-supply and plan limits (6.5%), other (5.1%) and specialty or limited-distribution pharmacy requirements (2.4%). There were no step-therapy rejections.
4. **Who is affected:** new and continuing patients are rejected at about the same rate (18.1% vs 18.5%). The 10 mL pack's higher rate comes mostly from more prior-auth rejections (12.8% of its claims vs 9.6% for the 5 mL).

**What happens after a rejection:** 25% of rejected scripts were abandoned (540) and 37% were switched to another product (805): Tearsol 386, generic cyclosporine 188, Cyclomist 141 and Hydrova 90. None were switched to the company's own Cyclosporine OS. Most rejections therefore end up as lost patients or volume going to competitors. The best places to act are PA renewal support at Northstar, Bluecrest and Redwood, and formulary talks with Harborview, Evergreen and Granite Plains.
