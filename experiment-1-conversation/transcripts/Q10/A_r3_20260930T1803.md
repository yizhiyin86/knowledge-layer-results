**Sources and definitions**
- **Supply data (Fabric):** `dbo.BOM`, exploded through all levels (5 is the deepest). I kept only BOM lines in effect on 2026-09-30. Materials came from `dbo.MATERIAL`, NDC links from `dbo.MATERIAL_NDC_XREF`, and suppliers from `dbo.SUPPLIER_MATERIAL` plus `dbo.SUPPLIER`.
- **Single-source:** a purchased material with exactly one supplier marked as qualified (`QUALIFIED_FLG='Y'`). Unqualified suppliers don't count as a backup.
- **Commercial data (Snowflake):** "Our products" means `CORE.PRODUCT` rows with `CO_FLG='Y'` (5 NDCs). TRx is `SUM(TRX_QTY)` from `RAW_3P.RXT_WKLY` over the 13 most recent weeks, all channels.
- **Combining the two:** there's no cross-database join, so I matched the platforms by hand on the NDC, with dashes removed on the supply side.

**Assumptions and caveats**
- "TRx at risk" is the full recent TRx of any product with at least one single-source dependency. It's an exposure figure, not an estimate of the shortfall from a specific disruption. I didn't factor in inventory on hand or safety stock.
- Items made in-house (bulk, filled bottles and the pack kit) have no suppliers, so they aren't counted as single-source. Each is made at only one plant, though: PL-01 for cyclosporine, PL-02 for Oculon. That's a separate concentration risk.
- The other 55 finished-goods codes in the supply system (the FG-3xxx series) aren't in the commercial product master and have no TRx data, so they're out of scope.
