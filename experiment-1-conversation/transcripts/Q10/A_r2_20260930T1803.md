- For scale, 16,554 TRx per 13 weeks works out to roughly 66K TRx a year, if the current run-rate holds.

- **BOM (Fabric `dbo.BOM`):** only lines in effect on 2026-09-30, followed down all levels (it goes 5 levels deep; 281 lines had expired). Fabric doesn't support recursive queries, so I followed each level with explicit joins. I did not use `dbo.BOM_FLAT_LEGACY`, which was last synced 2024-11-30.
- **Single-source:** a purchased material with exactly one supplier marked qualified (`QUALIFIED_FLG = 'Y'`) in `dbo.SUPPLIER_MATERIAL`. Unqualified suppliers don't count as a second source. Supplier names and countries come from `dbo.SUPPLIER`. Materials we make in-house (the kit, filled bottles and bulk intermediates) have no supplier, so I assessed their inputs instead.
- **Product mapping:** `dbo.MATERIAL_NDC_XREF`, with dashes removed from the NDCs, matched to Snowflake `CORE.PRODUCT`. "Our products" means `CO_FLG = 'Y'` (5 NDCs). The FG-30xx lines (lens care, supplements and so on) have NDCs but no rows in the prescription data, so I left them out.
- **TRx:** sum of `TRX_QTY` in `PHARMA_DEMO.RAW_3P.RXT_WKLY` for the 13 most recent weeks (week ending 2026-07-03 through 2026-09-25). All 13 weeks are present, and there are no duplicate rows, nulls or negative values.
- **Assumptions:**
  - "Volume at risk" means the full TRx of each affected product, not a modeled estimate of how much supply would actually be lost.
  - I didn't account for inventory buffers or supplier concentration beyond the named single sources. For example, S-104 also supplies benzalkonium chloride for Oculon, but that material has a second qualified supplier, S-130.
  - I only used aggregated product-level TRx, with no prescriber-level data, which is appropriate for a supply-planning request.
