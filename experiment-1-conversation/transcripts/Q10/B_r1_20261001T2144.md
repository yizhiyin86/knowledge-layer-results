**What this means for planning**
- **Lacrivia and Cyclosporine OS (13,986 TRx) share one supply chain.** Polysorbate 80 (RM-221) goes into the emulsifier premix (CMP-310), which goes into the shared bulk SEMI-031. Both materials have only one qualified supplier. RM-221 has a second supplier on file (S-131), but it is not qualified.
- **Lacrivia would have no company substitute.** Under rule BR-SUB-01, Cyclosporine OS is the approved substitute for Lacrivia only if it isn't hit by the same supply problem. Here it is. If RM-221 or CMP-310 fails, both lose supply at once and Medical Affairs would guide alternatives.
- **Oculon 2.5 mL has no substitute either.** It has no approved company substitute and its bottle has no alternate supplier. Oculon 5 mL is not exposed, so it is a possible pack-size fallback. Treat that as a suggestion to confirm with Medical and Supply, not an approved substitute.

Between them they depend on 16 more single-source materials, e.g. RM-10857, RM-10360, RM-10211, RM-11172 and CMP-50757. These products are not in the commercial prescription data, so their TRx risk can't be measured.
- **Not all single-source materials matter today.** 24 materials have exactly one qualified supplier. Only 20 of them reach a finished good through the current BOM. The rest (e.g. RM-10514, which only feeds formulations that have since been replaced) don't.
