What I can show is where the numbers have to come from and how much the answer moves with the definition, which is enough to say the two reports probably can't be built the same way.

**Where "paid rate" can come from**
- **Only source with paid/rejected status: the claims table (`RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ`).** It has 287,395 claims, one row per claim ID, dated 27 Sep 2025 to 30 Sep 2026. Each claim is marked P (paid), R (rejected) or V (reversed). Every rejected claim has a reason code, and all of them match the reject-code lookup.
- **Lookups:** the reject-code table groups reasons (e.g. prior auth, not covered, step therapy). The payer table rolls plans up to payer, PBM and channel. The product table maps NDCs to brands.
- **Paid claims feed the weekly prescription counts (`RAW_3P.RXT_WKLY`).** Over the last 13 weeks, total prescriptions there are 59,072, exactly the number of paid claims. So that table carries paid volume by prescriber, but no rejects.
- **Patient-level file (`RAW_3P.PATHPT_LVL`):** has the prescriber and an abandonment flag, but no paid/rejected status.

**Why the two reports probably differ**
- The claims table has no prescriber field. A payer view can calculate paid ÷ (paid + rejected) directly from claims, by plan, payer or channel.
- A rep or territory view has no prescriber-level rejects to divide by. It would have to use paid prescription counts over some other base, or use "not abandoned" as a stand-in. Those are different metrics. For example, 94.7% of patient-level records were not abandoned, versus an 87.0% paid rate.

Claim counts: 59,072 paid, 8,834 rejected, 1,544 reversed.

| Paid ÷ (paid + rejected + reversed) | 85.1% | 80.0% |
| (Paid − reversed) ÷ (paid + rejected) | 84.7% | 79.6% |
| Primary payer only, or new patients only | 87.0% / 87.2% | 81.6% / 81.7% |
| Not abandoned, patient-level file (not a paid rate) | 94.7% | – |

So reversals alone move the rate by about 2 points. Using abandonment instead moves it by about 8.

**Traps that would also make the reports disagree**
1. **Backup table double-counts.** `RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ_BKP_2025` holds 77,757 claims from 27 Sep–31 Dec 2025. All of them are already in the main claims table, unchanged. Adding the two together double-counts that quarter.
2. **Recent days look better than they are.** Rejected and reversed claims stop on 25 Sep 2026, but paid claims run to 30 Sep. Counting 26–30 Sep adds 85 paid claims with no rejects, which pushes the rate up.
3. **Abandonment isn't the opposite of paid.** Every reversed claim and 2,256 rejected claims are flagged as abandoned. So "not abandoned" and "paid" measure different things.

**What would settle it:** the report owners' SQL or semantic-model definitions for numerator, denominator, how reversals are handled, which dates are included, and how prescribers are linked. Once I have them I can rebuild both reports' numbers from the table above.
