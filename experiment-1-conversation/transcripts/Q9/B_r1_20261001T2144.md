## Final answer

**No, the two reports define paid rate differently.** Both use the same source data. The only difference is how they treat reversed claims, and that makes the Rep Report about 2 points lower.

**Source:** Both versions are calculated from one table and one field: **`PHARMA_DEMO.RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ.ADJ_STAT`** (P = paid, R = rejected, V = reversed). To break it out by payer or channel, join `CORE.PAYER_SPINE` (on PLAN_ID) and then `REF.CHNL` (on CHNL_CD). Don't use `RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ_BKP_2025`; it's deprecated and replaced by `CLMLNS_ADJ`.

**How each report defines it:**

| | Payer Report: `paid_rate` (ratified) | Rep Report: `paid_rate_rep_v1` (not ratified, legacy) |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | paid / (paid + rejected) | paid / all claim lines |
| Reversals (V) | Left out of the denominator | Counted in the denominator, so they act as "not paid" |
| Owner / status | Ratified by Market Access | Not ratified |

**What the difference looks like in the data** (all claims, 2025-09-27 to 2026-09-30: 287,395 lines; 244,448 paid, 36,258 rejected, 6,689 reversed):

- Payer Report definition: **87.08%**
- Rep Report definition: **85.06%**, which is **2.0 points lower**

| Channel | Payer Report | Rep Report | Gap (points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial | 86.38% | 84.42% | 1.96 |
| Medicare Part D | 85.96% | 84.00% | 1.96 |
| Managed Medicaid | 88.34% | 86.23% | 2.11 |
| Health Exchange | 83.99% | 82.07% | 1.92 |
| Medicaid FFS | 89.45% | 87.25% | 2.20 |
| Cash / Discount | 100.00% | 96.95% | 3.05 |

The gap is about 2 points in every insured channel. It's largest for Cash / Discount, which has no rejections, so reversals are the only thing pulling its Rep Report number below 100%. Because of this, the two reports can't be compared directly. I'd use the ratified `paid_rate` as the standard and either move the Rep Report onto it or clearly label its figure as "paid / all claim lines".
