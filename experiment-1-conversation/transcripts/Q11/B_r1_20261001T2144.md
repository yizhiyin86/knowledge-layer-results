No. S-104 (Delmar Specialty Chemicals, India, tier 2) is the primary qualified supplier for two raw materials. Only one of them has a qualified alternate.

| Material | Qualified alternate? | Finished goods it feeds (current BOM) | Status if S-104 is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RM-222** Benzalkonium chloride 50% | **Yes.** S-130 Oakridge Chemicals (US) is qualified. It already ships this material: 11 closed POs (45,700 units) and 1 open PO (3,700 units). | Oculon 0.005% 2.5 mL (FG-2001) and 5 mL (FG-2002) | **Covered.** Oculon is not exposed through S-104. |
| **RM-221** Polysorbate 80 NF, low-peroxide | **No.** The only other listed source, S-131 Pinecrest Chemical Trading (China), is **not qualified** and has no PO history. | 14 finished goods (below) | **Exposed.** Only S-104 can supply it. |

**Products that would stay exposed (all fed by RM-221):**
- **Lacrivia 0.1% ophthalmic emulsion, 5 mL** (FG-1001, NDC 99101-0101-05) and **10 mL** (FG-1002, NDC 99101-0101-10). RM-221 is 5 levels below them: RM-221 → CMP-310 → SEMI-031 → SEMI-044 → fill → finished good.
- **Cyclosporine 0.05% ophthalmic emulsion, 5 mL** (FG-1003, NDC 99101-0202-05). It goes through the same CMP-310 / SEMI-031 chain.
- **Lens care solutions 1–11** (FG-3001 to FG-3011, NDCs 99101-4001-91 to 99101-4011-15). RM-221 is 7 levels below them, via INT-LC1011 → INT-LC2002. They aren't in the commercial (Snowflake) database, so they have no prescription or sales data.

**What this means:**
- **No company substitute for Lacrivia.** The approved substitute rule (BR-SUB-01) names Cyclosporine OS 0.05% as the substitute. But it depends on the same RM-221 chain, so it fails too. Medical Affairs would have to guide therapeutic alternatives.
- **Open orders at risk:** S-104 has 2 open POs for RM-221 (7,500 units) and 1 for RM-222 (2,600 units). The RM-222 volume could be moved to S-130. The RM-221 volume can't be moved anywhere.
- **Second single-source risk on the same path:** CMP-310, the emulsifier premix (polysorbate 80 / castor oil), has only one listed supplier, S-201. Even with a second polysorbate 80 source, the Lacrivia and Cyclosporine chain would still depend on S-201.
- **Suggested next step:** start qualifying S-131 or another supplier for RM-221. Until then, track Lacrivia and Cyclosporine OS days of supply. Recovery after any disruption includes a 14-day sterility test for ophthalmics (BR-LEAD-01); `get_lead_times` gives the full timeline.

**Sources and definitions**
- Supplier coverage: `dbo.SUPPLIER_MATERIAL` and `dbo.SUPPLIER` from the Supply Network data product. A supplier counts as an alternate only if `QUALIFIED_FLG = 'Y'`, which is the ratified `qualified_supplier_count` metric (1 means single-source).
- Exposure: `trace_supply_exposure` for S-104, filtered to RM-221 and RM-222. It walks only BOM edges that are still in effect and ignores the deprecated `BOM_FLAT_LEGACY`. It skipped an expired RM-221 → CMP-320 edge (ended 2025-06-30), so Oculon isn't exposed through RM-221.
- Open orders: `dbo.PURCH_ORD`, not the deprecated archive table.
- Rules: BR-SUB-01 (approved substitutes) and BR-LEAD-01 (recovery lead time). The policy check showed no restrictions for the SUPPLY_PLANNER role.
