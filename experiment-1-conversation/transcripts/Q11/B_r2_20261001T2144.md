**Products that are covered**
- Oculon 0.005% 2.5 mL (FG-2001) and 5 mL (FG-2002) depend on S-104 only through RM-222. S-130 is a qualified alternate, so they would not be stranded.
- Switching to S-130 still means moving volume to a non-primary source. Its capacity and transit time still need to be confirmed.

**Additional risks**
- **No company substitute for Lacrivia:** Company rules say Cyclosporine 0.05% is the approved substitute during a Lacrivia shortage, unless the same supply problem affects it. Here it does (FG-1003 is fed by the same RM-221 and CMP-310 chain). So there would be no approved company substitute, and Medical Affairs would need to guide therapeutic alternatives.
- **Second single-source point on the same path:** CMP-310, the emulsifier premix, has only one supplier (S-201), so the eye emulsions have two single-source points in a row.
- **Fastest fix:** Qualifying S-131 for RM-221 is the main way to close the gap. Until then, RM-221 stock and recovery lead times for FG-1001 to FG-1003 are worth checking. Those lead times include the 14-day sterility test for eye products.
