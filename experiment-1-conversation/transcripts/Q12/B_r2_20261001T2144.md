## Final answer

**Short answer:** The two-week delay doesn't change when DC-EAST runs out of Lacrivia, because it already has. It does push recovery back by 14 days, from about 26 Nov to about **10 Dec 2026**. Until then, the rules say Tier A health systems get stock first, then Tier B, then everyone else pro rata. There is no approved company substitute.

**1. When DC-EAST runs out**
| Pack | On hand 25 Sep | Safety stock | Average shipped per day (last 28 days) | Days of supply | Projected stock-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FG-1001 Lacrivia 5 mL | 46 | 471 | 36.7 | 1.3 | **about 26 Sep 2026** |
| FG-1002 Lacrivia 10 mL | 34 | 233 | 15.1 | 2.3 | **about 27 Sep 2026** |

As of the 30 Sep data date, DC-EAST is effectively out of both packs.

**2. When supply recovers**
- The next shipment is PO005791: 4,200 units of RM-221 (polysorbate 80, a raw material). S-104 promised to ship it on 7 Oct. Two weeks late means it ships **21 Oct**.
- It takes 50 days from shipment until finished Lacrivia reaches the DC (plant standard times):
  - 6 days transit to the S-201 premix site (arrives 27 Oct)
  - 10 days to make the CMP-310 premix (6 Nov)
  - 4 days for SEMI-031 (10 Nov)
  - 8 days for the SEMI-044 bulk emulsion (18 Nov)
  - 3 days to fill (21 Nov)
  - 1 day to pack, 14 days of sterility testing and QC release, and 4 days to the DC (**10 Dec**)
- So DC-EAST goes without Lacrivia for about 75 days, from late September to 10 Dec. At current demand that is a shortfall of about **2,750 units of 5 mL and 1,130 of 10 mL**. The delay alone adds about 510 and 210 units.
- The risk is real. S-104 shipped 11 of its 35 closed RM-221 orders in 2026 late, by up to 20 days. It is the only qualified source for RM-221: the alternate, S-131, is not qualified.
