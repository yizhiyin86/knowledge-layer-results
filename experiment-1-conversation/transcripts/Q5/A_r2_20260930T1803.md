## Final answer

**Answer:** When a Lacrivia claim is rejected, only about 1 in 3 ends in a switch to another product. When a switch does happen, Tearsol (lifitegrast) is the main destination at about half of switches. Generic cyclosporine is second at about a quarter.

**Where rejected Lacrivia claims go (27 Sep 2025 – 25 Sep 2026)**
Lacrivia had 51,092 claims, and 9,199 were rejected (18.0%).

| Outcome after rejection | Claims | % of rejects | % of switches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tearsol** (lifitegrast, competitor) | 1,630 | 17.7% | **49.6%** |
| **Cyclosporine generic** 0.05% | 820 | 8.9% | **24.9%** |
| **Cyclomist** (cyclosporine 0.09%, competitor) | 497 | 5.4% | **15.1%** |
| **Hydrova** (perfluorohexyloctane, competitor) | 340 | 3.7% | **10.3%** |
| No switch recorded | 5,912 | 64.3% | — |
| *of which the patient abandoned the fill* | *2,231* | *24.3%* | — |

**Key points**
- **All switches leave our portfolio.** No rejected Lacrivia claim moved to Cyclosporine OS, our own 0.05% product. Three-quarters of switches go to branded competitors; the rest go to generic cyclosporine.
- **The pattern holds across reject reasons and channels.** Tearsol is always first, at about 17–18% of rejects. Generic cyclosporine is always second, at about 9–10%, except in the "Other" reject group, where Cyclomist ties it. This is true for Commercial, Part D, Managed Medicaid, Medicaid FFS and Exchange plans alike.
- **Prior authorization causes most of it.** PA rejects are 5,376 of the 9,199 rejects (58%) and 1,892 of the switches. Not-covered rejects are next, with 1,925 rejects and 701 switches. No step-therapy (code 608) rejects were recorded for Lacrivia.
- **The last 13 weeks (Jul–Sep 2026) look much the same:** Tearsol 18.0%, generic 8.6%, Cyclomist 6.5% and Hydrova 4.1% of rejects, with no switch in 62.8%. Cyclomist is up slightly from 5.0% earlier in the year, and Hydrova from 3.6%.
- **Implication for market access:** the biggest loss is to Tearsol after PA rejections. Getting PAs approved, and keeping patients on our portfolio when coverage fails, would protect the most volume.

**Sources and definitions**
- Claims: `PHARMA_DEMO.RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ`. One row per claim, with no duplicate claim IDs.
  - "Rejected" means status `ADJ_STAT = 'R'`. Reversed claims (`'V'`) are left out.
  - "Switched to" means the substitute NDC recorded on the rejected claim (`SUBST_NDC11`).
  - Abandonment comes from `ABNDN_FLG`.
- Lacrivia means both of its NDCs: 99101010105 (5 mL) and 99101010110 (10 mL). Brands come from `CORE.PRODUCT`, reject groups from `REF.REJ_CODE` and channels from `CORE.PAYER_SPINE` plus `REF.CHNL`. Every code matched its reference table.

**Assumptions**
- These are claim counts, not unique patients, so a patient rejected more than once is counted more than once. I used only claim-level fields and did not link individual patient histories (`PATHPT_LVL`), which fits aggregate reporting for Market Access.
- "No switch recorded" may include later approvals, appeals or fills outside this data. The switch rate is therefore a floor.
- "Last 13 weeks" is approximated as claims dated 1 Jul 2026 or later. The newest rejected claim is dated 25 Sep 2026.
- No supply-chain (Fabric) data was needed.
