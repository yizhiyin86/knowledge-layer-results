## Final answer

Over the past 12 months, about 36% of rejected Lacrivia claims ended with the patient switched to another product. Every switch went to a competitor. Lifitegrast (Tearsol) took about half of them, and cyclosporine products (the generic plus Cyclomist) took about 40%.

**Lacrivia rejected claims, 27 Sep 2025 to 25 Sep 2026 (9,199 rejections)**

| Outcome after rejection | Claims | % of all rejections | % of switches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Switched to Tearsol** (lifitegrast) | 1,630 | 17.7% | 49.6% |
| **Switched to generic cyclosporine** | 820 | 8.9% | 24.9% |
| **Switched to Cyclomist** (cyclosporine) | 497 | 5.4% | 15.1% |
| **Switched to Hydrova** (perfluorohexyloctane) | 340 | 3.7% | 10.3% |
| **All switches** | **3,287** | **35.7%** | 100% |
| No switch, prescription abandoned | 2,231 | 24.3% | – |
| No switch, not abandoned | 3,681 | 40.0% | – |

**What stands out**
- **No switches went to our own products.** All four products patients moved to are competitors. Not one rejected Lacrivia claim was switched to our own Cyclosporine OS 0.05%.
- **The switch pattern is about the same whatever the rejection reason.** Between 32% and 39% of rejections end in a switch in every reason group, and Tearsol is always the top destination:

| Rejection reason | Rejections | Switched | Switch rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior authorization | 5,376 | 1,892 | 35.2% |
| Not covered | 1,925 | 701 | 36.4% |
| Refill | 695 | 263 | 37.8% |
| Plan limit | 563 | 220 | 39.1% |
| Other | 448 | 150 | 33.5% |
| Distribution limit | 192 | 61 | 31.8% |

- **Prior authorization is where most patients are lost.** It accounts for 58% of rejections and 58% of switches (1,892), so making prior authorization easier at the biggest plans would recover the most patients.
- **Losses go beyond switches.** About 60% of rejected claims ended with no Lacrivia fill: 36% switched and 24% abandoned the prescription. No claims were rejected for step therapy in this period.

**Sources and definitions**
- Claims table: `PHARMA_DEMO.RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ`. I counted claims with `ADJ_STAT='R'` (rejected), and the product switched to comes from `SUBST_NDC11`.
- Lacrivia's codes and each product's name and company flag come from the product master, `PHARMA_DEMO.CORE.PRODUCT` (`BRAND_NM='LACRIVIA'`).
- Rejection reasons come from `PHARMA_DEMO.REF.REJ_CODE`, using the knowledge layer's approved "Claim to ClaimRejection group" join.
- The abandonment figure is the ratified metric `abandonment_after_rejection`.
- I used the current claims table, not the deprecated `CLMLNS_ADJ_BKP_2025`. No data-use restrictions applied to these tables for the Market Access role.

**Assumptions**
- The period is all available claims, about 12 months.
- A rejection counts as a switch only if a switch was recorded on it. Rejections with no switch and no abandonment may have been resolved later (for example, approved on appeal). This data can't tell us that.
- Counts are claim lines, not individual patients, because the data has no patient IDs.
