## Final answer

When a Lacrivia claim is rejected, the patient is switched to another product about 36% of the time. **Tearsol (lifitegrast)** takes about half of those switches. None of the switches go to our other dry-eye product, Cyclosporine OS, so every switch is lost to a competitor or a generic.

**Lacrivia rejected claims, 27 Sep 2025 – 25 Sep 2026 (9,199 in total)**

| Outcome / product switched to | Claims | % of all rejects | % of switches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Switched (total)** | **3,287** | **35.7%** | 100% |
| • Tearsol (lifitegrast, competitor) | 1,630 | 17.7% | 49.6% |
| • Cyclosporine generic 0.05% | 820 | 8.9% | 24.9% |
| • Cyclomist (cyclosporine 0.09%, competitor) | 497 | 5.4% | 15.1% |
| • Hydrova (perfluorohexyloctane, competitor) | 340 | 3.7% | 10.3% |
| Abandoned (no switch) | 2,231 | 24.3% | – |
| Rejected, no switch or abandonment recorded | 3,681 | 40.0% | – |

**What drives it**
- **Reject reason:** Prior-authorization rejects are 58% of all Lacrivia rejects (5,376) and account for 1,892 switches, 952 of them to Tearsol. The next biggest reason is "not covered or not on formulary" (1,925 rejects, 36% switch). The switch rate is similar for every reason, from 32% to 39%. The highest is plan limits (39%) and the lowest is limited distribution (32%). None of the Lacrivia rejects carried a step-therapy code.
- **Channel:** Commercial has the most rejects (4,461; 35% switch), then Medicare Part D (2,431; 36%). Medicaid FFS has the highest switch rate at 40%, but on only 308 rejects. The order of products switched to is the same in every channel: Tearsol, then the generic, then Cyclomist, then Hydrova.
- **Trend:** In the last 13 weeks the switch rate rose to 37.5%, up from 35.2% before that. Tearsol's share of switches fell slightly (48% vs 50%) and Cyclomist's rose (18% vs 14%).

**Sources and definitions**
- **Claims:** `PHARMA_DEMO.RAW_3P.CLMLNS_ADJ`. A rejection is `ADJ_STAT = 'R'`. A switch is a rejected claim with a substitute product code filled in (`SUBST_NDC11`). An abandonment is a rejected claim with no substitute and the abandon flag set to Y.
- **Lacrivia:** both package codes, 0.1% 5 mL and 10 mL, from `CORE.PRODUCT`. Substitute codes were decoded against the same table and all matched.
- **Reject reasons:** decoded with `REF.REJ_CODE`. Channels come from `CORE.PAYER_SPINE` plus `REF.CHNL`; every claim mapped.

**Assumptions and caveats**
- The period is all the claims data available, about 12 months. "Last 13 weeks" means claims after 1 Jul 2026.
- Switching is taken from the substitute product recorded on the rejected claim itself. I didn't track patients across later claims because the patient-level table (`PATHPT_LVL`) has no field to link it to claims. The 40% of rejects with nothing recorded may include some patients who switched or were re-approved later.
- Reversed claims (`ADJ_STAT = 'V'`) are not rejections and are left out.
- I found no data-use restrictions on these tables for the Market Access role. All results are aggregated and no patient-level information is shown.
