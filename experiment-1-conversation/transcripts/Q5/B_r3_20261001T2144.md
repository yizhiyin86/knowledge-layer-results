## Final answer

Over the past year, about 36% of rejected Lacrivia claims ended in a switch to another product, and every one of those switches went to a competitor. Tearsol (lifitegrast) took half of them. None went to our own Cyclosporine OS.

**Where the 9,199 rejected Lacrivia claims went (claims dated 27 Sep 2025 – 25 Sep 2026, both Lacrivia packs)**

| Outcome | Claims | % of rejections | % of switches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tearsol** (lifitegrast, competitor) | 1,630 | 17.7% | 49.6% |
| **Generic cyclosporine** 0.05% (competitor) | 820 | 8.9% | 24.9% |
| **Cyclomist** (cyclosporine 0.09%, competitor) | 497 | 5.4% | 15.1% |
| **Hydrova** (perfluorohexyloctane, competitor) | 340 | 3.7% | 10.3% |
| **Switched, total** | **3,287** | **35.7%** | 100% |
| Abandoned, no switch | 2,231 | 24.3% | – |
| No switch and not abandoned | 3,681 | 40.0% | – |

**Last 13 weeks (claims after 26 Jun 2026):** 878 of 2,367 rejections (37.1%) were switched. The mix hasn't changed much: Tearsol 48.7%, generic cyclosporine 23.2%, Cyclomist 17.0%, Hydrova 11.0%.

**By rejection reason:** the pattern is about the same for every reason, at roughly 32–39% switched.
- **Prior authorisation:** largest reason, with 5,376 rejections (58%); 1,892 switched (35%), and 952 of those went to Tearsol.
- **Not covered:** 1,925 rejections; 36% switched.
- **Plan limit:** 563 rejections; highest switch rate at 39%.
- **Distribution limit:** 192 rejections; lowest switch rate at 32%, but abandonment without a switch is high at 30%.

**What this means for market access:** prior-authorisation rejections account for the most patients lost to competitors, mostly to Tearsol. Our own Cyclosporine OS 0.05% (approved as a substitute only during a Lacrivia shortage) currently gets none of these switches. Patients who move to a cyclosporine are going to the generic or to Cyclomist instead.
