**What this means for Market Access**
- **Commercial is driving the growth.** Weekly share rose from 23.1% to 29.3%. The whole dry-eye market's Commercial volume stayed flat (21.5K vs 21.8K TRx), so Lacrivia is gaining actual prescriptions, not just holding on in a shrinking market. It took share mainly from the cyclosporine generic, Hydrova and Tearsol. This fits a formulary or access win, but I didn't check the formulary data to confirm that.
- **Medicare Part D is the concern.** Weekly share fell from about 22% to about 18% over the quarter while Tearsol gained. Part D is the second-largest channel (about 25% of market TRx), so this offsets part of the Commercial gain. It's worth checking Part D formulary tier and prior-authorization or rejection changes.
- The Medicaid FFS and Health Exchange figures rest on small weekly volumes. Rely on the 13-week totals rather than week-to-week moves.

**Sources and definitions**
- **Main source:** `PHARMA_DEMO.MART.PYR_SHR_WK`, filtered to the dry-eye market and to Lacrivia. I rebuilt it from the raw weekly prescription data (`RAW_3P.RXT_WKLY`) using the product master (`CORE.PRODUCT`) and the market-definition file (`BIZ.MKT_DEF_DRIVER`, v7). All 52 weeks and 6 channels matched exactly, both for Lacrivia's TRx and for the market's.
- **Market definition:** the dry-eye market has six brands: Lacrivia (both 5 mL and 10 mL packs), Tearsol, generic cyclosporine, Cyclomist, Hydrova and Cyclosporine OS. Share is Lacrivia TRx divided by total dry-eye market TRx. Multi-week shares are total TRx divided by total TRx, not an average of weekly shares.
- **Channels:** decoded with `REF.CHNL`. Every prescription row's plan maps to `CORE.PAYER_SPINE`, and the channel codes agree.
- **Time periods:** the "last 13 weeks" are the 13 latest weekly periods (week ending 3 Jul to 25 Sep 2026). The prior 13 weeks run from 3 Apr to 26 Jun 2026. The latest week looks complete, with no sign of a data lag. "pts/week" is the slope of a straight line fitted to the 13 weekly shares.
- **Tables not used:** `MART.PYR_SHR_WK_OLD` (older logic) has a larger total market than the dry-eye definition (for example, 2,251 vs 1,711 Commercial TRx in its first week), so its shares are lower. I also didn't use the sandbox scratch tables, including `LACRIVIA_SHR_SCRATCH`.
- **Data used:** only aggregated TRx counts. No patient-level or prescriber-level data was used or shown.
