## Final answer

DC-EAST is not just at risk of a Lacrivia stockout; it is almost certainly out of stock already, or will be within days.** Both Lacrivia packs fell well below safety stock in September and had less than 3 days of supply at the last inventory snapshot (25 Sep). Meanwhile, prescription demand (TRx) in DC-EAST's states is rising, so the true days of supply are even shorter.

**DC-EAST inventory (snapshot 2026-09-25)**

| Pack (material) | On hand | Safety stock | Units shipped, last 28 days | Days of supply |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lacrivia 0.1% 5 mL (FG-1001) | 46 | 471 | 1,027 (≈36.7/day) | **1.3 days** |
| Lacrivia 0.1% 10 mL (FG-1002) | 34 | 233 | 423 (≈15.1/day) | **2.3 days** |

- **How fast stock fell:** stock was stable or slightly rising through 14 Aug (5 mL: 1,227; 10 mL: 572), then dropped every week. The 5 mL went 1,121 → 988 → 841 → 302 → 177 → 46.
- **Replenishment hasn't kept up:** shipments to wholesalers held at about 240–280 units a week for the 5 mL, so incoming supply has fallen far below what goes out.
- **An unexplained drop:** between 4 and 11 Sep, 5 mL stock fell by 539 units, but only about 280 were shipped. A write-off or quality hold is possible and worth checking.
- **Timing:** at last week's net drop (about 130 units/week for 5 mL, about 73 for 10 mL), both packs would run out around 27 Sep – 1 Oct. That is on or before today (30 Sep).

**The demand trend makes it worse.** These are Lacrivia TRx from prescribers in the DC-EAST states (NY, NJ, PA, MA, FL, GA, NC, OH):
- **Last 13 weeks (3 Jul – 25 Sep):** 5,170 TRx, about 398 a week. That is **+8.6%** on the 13 weeks before (4,761, about 366 a week).
- **Last 4 weeks:** about 424 a week, **+16%** on the earlier 13-week average. Every week in September was above 400.
- **Share of national demand:** DC-EAST's states make up about 49% of national Lacrivia TRx (426 of 867 last week).
- **Shipments lag prescriptions:** units shipped are flat (about 363 a week for both packs) while TRx rise. Wholesalers are probably drawing down their own stock, so the shortfall will reach pharmacies soon.
